# Axis 1 — the textbook proofs

## 1. The theorems

For the mathematical statements, manifolds are Hausdorff, second countable, smooth
($C^\infty$), finite-dimensional over $\mathbb R$, and boundaryless unless explicitly
stated. In particular they admit countable chart covers and compact exhaustions.
The Lean source uses `ModelWithCorners` and sometimes weaker explicit hypotheses:
`ChartedSpace` alone does not imply these countability or separation properties.
Preserve the actual per-theorem instances in the ledger, including `LindelofSpace X`
or `LindelofSpace (X × Y)` in the noncompact Sard/general-position statements.
"Dimension" means the finite real dimension of the model. For maps
$f \colon X \to N$, $g \colon Y \to N$ between manifolds, write $f \pitchfork g$
(*transverse at $(x, y)$*) for: $f(x) = g(y)$ implies
$\mathrm{im}\, df_x + \mathrm{im}\, dg_y = T_{f(x)} N$; and $f \pitchfork g$ for: this holds at
all pairs.

**(T1) Sard's theorem (equal-dimensional form; Hirsch Ch. 3 Thm. 1.3 pattern).** For a smooth
map $f \colon X \to F$ from an $n$-manifold to an $n$-dimensional vector space, the set of
regular values is comeager and co-null: there is a Haar-null set $T \subseteq F$ such that at
every $x$ with $f(x) \notin T$, $df_x$ is surjective.

**(T2) Parametric transversality (translation form; Hirsch Ch. 3 Thm. 2.1).** For smooth
$f \colon X \to F$, $g \colon Y \to F$ into a vector space with
$\dim X + \dim Y = \dim F$, the set of translation parameters $a \in F$ for which
$f \pitchfork (g + a)$ is dense (indeed co-null).

**(T3) Ambient transversality (Hirsch Ch. 3 Thm. 2.5 form).** Let $X$ be compact, $Y$ compact,
$N$ boundaryless, $f \colon X \to N$, $g \colon Y \to N$ smooth, with
$\dim X + \dim Y = \dim N$. Then there is a diffeomorphism $e \colon N \to N$ isotopic to the
identity (through diffeomorphisms, smoothly in time) such that $(e \circ f) \pitchfork g$.

**(T4) General position (disjunction).** If $\dim X + \dim Y < \dim N$, $X$ compact, and the
range of $g$ is closed (e.g. $Y$ compact), then every
smooth $f \colon X \to N$ is homotopic rel any closed set $C$ already avoiding $g$ to a smooth
$f'$ with $\mathrm{range}\, f' \cap \mathrm{range}\, g = \varnothing$. For the ambient form
assume additionally that $Y$ is compact (as required by T3 after sphere padding):
there is an ambient diffeomorphism
isotopic to the identity with $\mathrm{range}(e \circ f) \cap \mathrm{range}\, g =
\varnothing$.

**(T5) Supported chart transport.** Let $\Phi:U\to V$ be a diffeomorphism between
open subsets of manifolds $X,Y$. A jointly smooth family of global diffeomorphisms
$A_t:X\to X$, starting at the identity and fixed outside one compact $K\subset U$,
transports through $\Phi$ and extends by the identity to an isotopy of $Y$, fixed outside
$\Phi(K)$. This is the source theorem promised by this lane. The classical extension
of an isotopy of a compact submanifold (Hirsch Ch. 8 Thm. 1.3) is a distinct theorem;
§7 describes its velocity-field argument as context, not an additional extracted output.

**(T6) Positive-codimension disc theorem.** Let $M$ be a compact manifold of dimension
at least 2,
$D$ a finite-dimensional inner-product space with $\dim D < \dim M$, and let
$f, g \colon D \to M$ be smooth, injective with injective derivatives on the closed unit ball,
with $f(0) = g(0)$. Then there is a diffeomorphism $P$ of $M$, isotopic to the identity, with
$P \circ f = g$ on the unit ball. (The strict inequality is necessary: at
$\dim D = \dim M$ the derivative frames of $f$ and $g$ must share a determinant sign, since an
isotopy from the identity has positive determinant throughout; the proof's normal-flip step
uses a spare normal direction. The code's statement carries the strict inequality. The
equal-dimensional case holds with the additional orientation-compatibility hypothesis, the
flip simply being unnecessary. These broader variants are context, not new outputs.
The current proof uses $2\leq\dim M$ to obtain a nontrivial ambient frame index in the
germ-alignment construction (Transversality/Basic.lean:2711), and compactness in the tubular provider;
both hypotheses are retained. Milnor's h-cobordism Thm. 9.6 is the related
orientation-compatible full-dimensional disc theorem, not literally this signature.)

**(T7) Homogeneity.** A connected boundaryless manifold is homogeneous under compactly
supported diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity: for $x, y$ in one connected component and
any neighborhood $U$ of a path from $x$ to $y$, there is such a diffeomorphism moving $x$ to
$y$ and fixed outside $U$. The extracted headline records isotopy to the identity
and endpoint fixedness outside $U$; the finite local construction explains the stronger
compact-support statement, but a public uniform-support witness would be a separate
API addition. The predicate `IsotopicToIdentity` itself has no support field.

**(T8) Relative Whitney immersion and embedding (Hirsch Ch. 2 Thms. 1.1, 1.3).** Let $X$ be a
$k$-manifold, $N$ an $n$-manifold, $f \colon X \to N$ smooth.
* If $2k + 1 \leq n$, then rel any closed set $C$ on a neighborhood of which $f$ is already
  immersive, $f$ is homotopic rel $C$ to a map immersive on any prescribed compact set:
  precisely, for compacts $K$ (where $f$ is already immersive) and $L$ with $L \cap C =
  \varnothing$, there is $g$ homotopic to $f$ rel $C$ with $g$ immersive on $K \cup L$.
* If $2k < n$ and $f$ is an immersion on a compact $K$, injective with injective derivative on
  $K \cap C$, then $f$ is homotopic rel $C$ to $g$ which is a closed embedding on $K$ and
  immersive on $K$.
(The extracted code has **model-vector-space sources**, not arbitrary source manifolds:
$\mathbb R^2$ with $n\geq5$, and $\mathbb R$ with $n\geq3$; the embedding-from-immersion
result already takes an arbitrary finite-dimensional source vector space. **G-E2**
generalizes immersion creation to such a vector space $E$, retaining $2k+1\leq n$ for
compatibility. The arbitrary-manifold formulation above is mathematical context; its
source-chart wrapper is deferred and is not part of the G-E2 target signature.)

**(T9) Maps into spheres of larger dimension (Milnor TDV §2 pattern).** A continuous map from a
compact manifold of dimension $< n$ to $S^n$ is nullhomotopic. In particular every map
$S^m \to S^n$ with $m < n$ is nullhomotopic.

**(T10) Finite transverse intersections.** Let $F \colon N \to M$, $G \colon P \to M$ be
embeddings of compact manifolds into a manifold with
$\dim N + \dim P = \dim M$, meeting transversely. Then $\mathrm{range}\, F \cap
\mathrm{range}\, G$ is finite.

The dimension argument is chartwise: smooth images in a finite-dimensional vector space

## 3. Sard's theorem (T1)

*Proof.* Cover the domain by countably many charts (Lindelöf); a countable union of null sets
is null, so it suffices to prove: for a smooth $f \colon U \to \mathbb{R}^n$ with
$U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ open, the set of critical values is null. The critical set is
$\{x : \det df_x = 0\}$; on it, the analytic Sard lemma
(`addHaar_image_eq_zero_of_det_fderivWithin_eq_zero`: the image under a differentiable map of a
set where the derivative's determinant vanishes is Haar-null) gives nullness of the image
directly; the complement is the claimed co-null set of regular values. It is dense (a co-null
set in a space with a nonzero-on-opens Haar measure is dense), indeed comeager: the critical
set is closed and the domain is $\sigma$-compact, so the critical set is a countable union of
compacts; each compact piece's critical-value image is compact and null, hence has empty
interior (Haar is positive on nonempty opens), hence is nowhere dense; so the critical values
are meager. $\square$

*Remark.* The manifold statement quantifies over an open $s \subseteq X$ and concludes
surjectivity of `mfderiv`; the chart form quantifies over differentiability on a set. Both are
the equal-dimensional case; the general Sard theorem ($\dim X > \dim F$ allowed) is not needed
by this lane and is not proved here.

## 4. Parametric transversality (T2)

*Proof.* Let $D := \dim X$, $Z := \dim Y$, $D + Z = \dim F =: m$. Consider the sheet-difference
map $\Phi \colon X \times Y \to F$, $\Phi(x, y) = g(y) - f(x)$. A point $(x, y)$ is critical
for $\Phi$ iff $df_x$ and $dg_y$ fail to jointly span — more precisely
$d\Phi_{(x,y)} = dg_y - df_x$ (as a map $T_xX \times T_yY \to F$) is surjective iff
$\mathrm{im}\, df_x + \mathrm{im}\, dg_y = F$, which is exactly transversality at $(x, y)$.
Now apply Sard (T1) to $\Phi$ (dimension check: $\dim(X \times Y) = D + Z = m = \dim F$): for
co-null-many $a \in F$, $a$ is a regular value of $\Phi$. But $a$ a regular value with
$\dim(X \times Y) = \dim F$ means: at every $(x, y)$ with $\Phi(x, y) = a$, $d\Phi$ is
surjective; and $\Phi(x, y) = a$ iff $g(y) = f(x) + a$. So for co-null — hence dense — many
$a$, the translates $f + a$ and $g$ are everywhere transverse (equivalently $f$ and $g - a$;
equivalently, replacing $a$ by $-a$ — negation preserves conullness —
$f \pitchfork (g + a)$, the stated form).
$\square$

## 5. Ambient transversality (T3)

*Proof.* The construction has three layers.

*The bump family.* In a chart ball $B \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, let $\beta \colon
\mathbb{R}^n \to [0, 1]$ be smooth, compactly supported, $\equiv 1$ on a smaller ball $B'$.
For a parameter $a \in \mathbb{R}^n$ define the compactly supported map
$e_a(y) = y + \beta(y) \cdot a$; for $\|a\| < (\sup \|d\beta\|)^{-1}$ its derivative
$\mathrm{id} + a \otimes d\beta$ is everywhere invertible and $e_a$ is injective (by
$\|e_a(y_1) - e_a(y_2)\| \geq (1 - \|a\| \sup\|d\beta\|)\,\|y_1 - y_2\|$) and proper (identity
off the support of $\beta$), hence a diffeomorphism; and $t \mapsto e_{ta}$ is a smooth isotopy
from $\mathrm{id}$ to $e_a$. Transported through a chart $\varphi \colon U \to \mathbb{R}^n$ of $N$
and extended by the identity, this gives a compactly supported ambient isotopy class on $N$,
fixed outside $\varphi^{-1}(\mathrm{supp}\,\beta)$.

*The plateau lemma.* Let $K \subseteq X$ be compact and $f(K)$ contained in the plateau
$\{\beta = 1\}$ of one chart. On that plateau $e_a(f(x)) = f(x) + a$ in chart coordinates, so
transversality of $(e_a \circ f)$ against $g$ at points of $K$ is exactly transversality of the
translated map — which holds for dense (co-null) small $a$ by parametric transversality (T2
applied in the chart — the hypothesis $\dim X + \dim Y = \dim N$ is exactly its dimension
equation — to $f|_{f^{-1}(U)}$ and $g|_{g^{-1}(U)}$; no meeting pairs are lost by the
restriction, since for $x \in K$ and $\|a\|$ small, $e_a(f(x)) = f(x) + a$ stays in the chart,
so any $y$ with $g(y) = e_a(f(x))$ lies in $g^{-1}(U)$).
Choose such an $a$ with $\|a\| < \varepsilon$.

*The patch induction.* Cover the compact $X$ by finitely many patch interiors, each the
plateau of a bump in a chart. Apply the plateau lemma successively: after step $i$,
transversality holds on $K_1 \cup \dots \cup K_i$; step $i+1$ perturbs by a bump whose
parameter is small enough that $e_a \to \mathrm{id}$ in the $C^1$ topology as $a \to 0$ (the
joint-surjectivity condition is open and uniform on the compact pair set
$(K_1 \cup \dots \cup K_i) \times Y$, with $Y$ compact), and small enough that the current map
still sends the not-yet-treated patches $K_j$ into their (open) plateau balls, so the plateau
lemma remains applicable at later steps; the previous region stays transverse. After finitely
many steps,
compose the small diffeomorphisms: the composite $e$ is isotopic to the identity (concatenate
the isotopies) and $(e \circ f) \pitchfork g$ everywhere. $\square$

*Corollary (disjunction, the second half of T4).* If $\dim X + \dim Y < \dim N$, pad: apply T3
to $f \circ \mathrm{pr}_1 \colon X \times S^d \to N$ (with $d$ chosen so the dimensions match,
$\dim X + d + \dim Y = \dim N$, possible since the sphere of any dimension is available; the
padded map is constant in the sphere factor, so its transversality against $g$ is equivalent to
disjointness of the ranges — at a meeting point the padded $f$ derivative has image
dimension at most $\dim X$ (it ignores the sphere factor); together with $dg$ the
coproduct image has dimension at most $\dim X + \dim Y < \dim N$, so the coproduct
derivative can never be surjective). Transversality of the
padded pair is vacuous, hence the ranges of $e \circ f$ and $g$ are disjoint. $\square$

## 6. General position, map form (T4, first half)

*Proof.* Work in patches as in §5 but perturb $f$ itself: it suffices (patch induction as
before, using a homotopy through the perturbations to keep the rel-$C$ condition) to perturb
$f$ on one chart compactly, by $f'(x) = f(x) + \beta(x) \cdot a$ in chart coordinates, keeping
$f' = f$ off the patch (so the rel-$C$ condition is preserved: on $C$ no change happens where
$C$ meets the patch — ensure the patch is disjoint from $C$ or $\beta$ vanishes near $C$; the
hypothesis "range $g$ already avoided on $C$" gives room, since avoidance is open where the
range of $g$ is closed). The
condition $f'(x) \notin \mathrm{range}\, g$ on the patch core is:
$a \neq (g(y) - f(x))/\beta(x)$ for $x$ in the patch core and $y \in Y$ — the bad parameter set
is contained in the image of the smooth map $(x, y) \mapsto (g(y) - f(x))/\beta(x)$ on
$(\text{patch core}) \times Y$, a smooth map from a
$(\dim X + \dim Y)$-dimensional source into $\mathbb{R}^n$ with $\dim X + \dim Y < n$: a
smooth image of a lower-dimensional manifold has $\dim_H < n$, hence empty interior, hence
dense complement. (On $\{\beta = 0\}$ the map is unperturbed; those points are covered by the
other patch cores.) So arbitrarily small good $a$ exist. $\square$

## 7. Compactly supported isotopy extension (T5)

*Proof (the form the code proves).* Let $\Phi:U\to V$ be a diffeomorphism of open
subsets of $X,Y$. Let $A:\mathbb R\times X\to X$ be jointly smooth, with every
$A_t$ a global diffeomorphism, $A_0=\mathrm{id}$, and every $A_t$ fixed outside one
compact $K\subset U$. Bijectivity and fixedness outside $K$ imply $A_t(K)=K$ and
$A_t(U)=U$: a point outside $K$ is already its own unique preimage. Define
$B_t=\Phi\circ A_t\circ\Phi^{-1}$ on $V$, and the identity on $Y\setminus\Phi(K)$.
These definitions agree on the open overlap $V\setminus\Phi(K)$. The sets cover $Y$,
so the glued family is jointly smooth. Transporting $A_t^{-1}$ gives the inverse of
each slice; $B_0$ is the identity and every slice is fixed outside the compact $\Phi(K)$.
This proves the source's supported chart-extension statement without a spurious
surjectivity claim for arbitrary local diffeomorphisms.

*Classical context, not an additional extracted output.* For a smooth isotopy of
embeddings $i_t:P\to M$ of a compact boundaryless submanifold, $t\in[0,1]$, define
velocity along the track by $V_t(i_t(p))=\partial_t i_t(p)$. Tubular neighborhoods of
the track in time times $M$, local extensions and a partition of unity extend this to
a smooth time-dependent ambient vector field. Choose a cutoff equal to one along
the compact track with support in one compact ambient neighborhood. Its flow exists
for the whole time interval, and uniqueness of integral curves gives
$F_t\circ i_0=i_t$. This is the classical isotopy-extension proof. It is not a
consequence of chart conjugation alone; a standalone typed theorem for it is deferred.
The extracted `IsotopicToIdentity` predicate records no support, and a
`SupportedRelativeIsotopy e K S` requires a separate proof of compactness of $K$
whenever compact support is used. $\square$

## 8. The disc theorem (T6)

*Proof.* Let $f, g \colon D \to M$ be as stated, $k = \dim D$, $n = \dim M \geq 2$ (and
$k < n$ in the application), $f(0) = g(0) = m$.

*Step 1 (thicken the full disks).* Choose a normal vector space $Z$ of dimension
$n-k>0$. Tubular trivializations over the contractible disks give full-dimensional
partial diffeomorphisms $\Phi,\Psi$ from neighborhoods of
$\overline B_D(0,1)\times\{0\}$ in $D\times Z$ into $M$, with
$\Phi(x,0)=f(x)$ and $\Psi(x,0)=g(x)$. Their centers agree. The source obtains these
from the embedded-closed-ball tubular provider (Transversality/Basic.lean:2711). It is these thickened
charts, not the positive-codimension slices alone, to which supported chart transport applies.

*Step 2 (align the germs).* In a common ambient chart at the center, the derivatives
of the thickened charts are invertible frames. Reverse one chosen normal basis vector
if necessary, leaving the zero section unchanged, so the transition has positive
determinant. A smooth path in $\mathrm{GL}^+(n)$ joins that derivative to the identity:
determinant-one matrices are products of transvections, and positive determinant is
handled by a positive scaling. Each path is realized near zero by cutting off the
linear velocity field $\dot A_t A_t^{-1}x$ and integrating it. The resulting flow
agrees with $A_t$ on a **smaller neighborhood** whose entire trajectory stays in the
cutoff plateau for $0\leq t\leq1$; such a neighborhood exists by compactness of the
parameter interval. Agreement on the entire original plateau is not required.
After this frame adjustment, the remaining full-dimensional transition germ has
identity derivative. On a ball of radius $r$, the displacement $h - \mathrm{id}$ is
$o(r)$, and its derivative $d(h - \mathrm{id})$ is $o(1)$. A rescaled cutoff has
derivative $O(1/r)$, so the derivative of the cut-off displacement is still $o(1)$.
(The source's normal correction in fact chooses a normal automorphism making the
determinant exactly 1 — `Transversality/Basic.lean:2158–2175` — rather than first
making the sign positive and then scaling; the routes are compatible, and the exact
helper packet should preserve the live determinant-one inputs.) Make its global Lipschitz constant
less than one; interpolation with the identity is then injective, locally invertible
and proper, hence globally a diffeomorphism. Transport through the ambient chart realizes the germ
by a supported ambient diffeomorphism $R$, isotopic to the identity, such that
$R(\Phi(x,0))=\Psi(x,0)$ for $x$ near zero. This is the source's supported germ
alignment, not extension by the identity from a lower-dimensional slice.

*Step 3 (shrink by ambient isotopies).* Choose $0<a\leq1$ such that the preceding
germ equality holds on $a\overline B_D(0,1)$. In each thickened chart choose an
ellipsoidal neighborhood of the full zero-section disk contained in its domain,
rescale the normal directions, and use the radial family
$R_t(v)=((1-t)+t\psi(\|v\|^2))v$, where $a\leq\psi\leq1$, $\psi=a$ on the
unit ball, $\psi=1$ outside a larger ball and $\psi'\geq0$. Its tangential and radial
eigenvalues are positive; it is proper and injective, hence a diffeomorphism, with
uniform compact support for $t\in[0,1]$. T5 transports these **ambient** shrinkings
to maps $P,Q$ of $M$ with
$P(\Phi(x,0))=\Phi(ax,0)$ and $Q(\Psi(x,0))=\Psi(ax,0)$ on the full unit disk
(Transversality/Basic.lean:2596). Smooth time reparametrization extends the isotopies to the source's
all-real-time convention if needed.

*Step 4 (unshrink).* Set $F=Q^{-1}\circ R\circ P$. For every $\|x\|\leq1$,
$F(f(x))=Q^{-1}(R(\Phi(ax,0)))=Q^{-1}(\Psi(ax,0))=g(x)$.
All three factors are isotopic to the identity. This is exactly the composition
and equality checked at Transversality/Basic.lean:2675. $\square$

## 9. Homogeneity (T7)

*Proof.* For $x \in U \subseteq M$ ($U$ open), consider the orbit of $x$ under
compactly supported diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity and fixed outside $U$.
This orbit lies in $U$. At each already-reached $y$, a chart ball inside $U$ and the
bump isotopy of §5 move $y$ to any sufficiently nearby point; composition shows
that the orbit is open in $U$. These orbits partition $U$ into open sets, so each
orbit also has open complement in $U$. A connected path image contained in $U$
therefore lies in one orbit. Equivalently, cover the path by finitely many such
balls inside $U$ and compose the local motions; their supports remain inside $U$.
For global connected homogeneity, take $U=M$. $\square$

## 10. Relative Whitney immersion and embedding (T8) — the generalization G-E2

This is the one place where the formalization is dimension-pinned and the textbook must supply
the general argument. The pinned engine is the affine-perturbation density lemma for maps of
the plane; the general statement and proof:

**Lemma (immersion creation, general $k$).** *Let $X$ be a $k$-manifold, $N$ an $n$-manifold
with $2k + 1 \leq n$, $f \colon X \to N$ smooth, $K, L \subseteq X$ compact, $C \subseteq X$
closed with $L \cap C = \varnothing$, and $f$ with injective derivative on $K$. Then there is
$g$, homotopic to $f$ rel $C$, immersive on $K \cup L$.*
(The disjointness prevents a fixed region from forcing a nonimmersive map on the
new target compact; the pinned source has exactly `(hdis : Disjoint L C)`. To reduce
from the classical relative form for a prescribed compact $T$, let $U$ be the open
immersive locus containing $T\cap C$. Choose a compact buffer $D\subset U$ with
$T\cap C\subset\operatorname{int}D$, using local compactness and the compactness of
$T\cap C$. Then $L=T\setminus\operatorname{int}D$ is compact and disjoint from $C$.
Apply the lemma with already-immersive compact $D$ (or $K\cup D$ if an additional
compact $K$ is to be retained) and target $L$. Never infer immersion on the closure
of an arbitrary immersive neighborhood. This is the construction at Immersion/Relative.lean:2035.)

*Proof.* Cover $L$ by finitely many chart patches compactly contained in chart domains; it
suffices to create the immersion property on one patch $P$ while keeping it on $K$ (open
condition, uniform on compacts) and not disturbing $C$. On the patch, in chart coordinates,
consider the affine perturbation family
$$g_a(x) = f(x) + \beta(x)\, A(x - x_0), \qquad a = A \in \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{R}^k,
\mathbb{R}^n),$$
with $\beta$ a cutoff $\equiv 1$ on the patch core, $x_0$ its center. Then
$dg_{A,x}(v)=df_x(v)+\beta(x)A(v)+d\beta_x(v)A(x-x_0)$.
On a neighborhood of the core, $\beta=1$ and $d\beta=0$, so this is **exactly** $df_x+A$.
The evaluation $(x,A)\mapsto df_x+A$ is a submersion, because its derivative in the
parameter $A$ is the identity. For each rank $r<k$, the rank-$r$ stratum in the space
of $n\times k$ matrices has codimension $(n-r)(k-r)\geq n-k+1$.
Its preimage under evaluation is a submanifold of dimension at most
$k+kn-(n-k+1)$. Projecting its countably many charts to parameter space gives a bad
parameter set of Hausdorff dimension at most this bound. The image need not itself
be a submanifold. The bound is less than $kn$ when
$k<n-k+1$, equivalently **$2k\leq n$** for integer dimensions. Thus the retained
stronger hypothesis $2k+1\leq n$ certainly suffices. For $k=0$ every derivative is
injective and the assertion is immediate, without rank strata.

Choose a sufficiently small good parameter. Smallness retains derivative injectivity
on the previously treated compact and keeps all target-chart compatibility conditions;
the cutoff is supported away from $C$, and the straight-line perturbation inside the
target chart gives a homotopy rel $C$. Finite induction over the patches of $L$ proves
the assertion. In the arbitrary-source-manifold formulation this is also a source-chart
induction; the currently designed Lean output specializes the source to a vector space.
$\square$

*Sanity check against the pins:* $k = 1$: $2 \cdot 1 + 1 = 3 \leq n$ — the curve chain's
`3 ≤ finrank ℝ G`. $k = 2$: $5 \leq n$ — the plane chain's `5 ≤ finrank ℝ G`. The
$k$-dimensional source in the code is the model space $\mathbb{R}^k$ written
`Plane := ℝ × ℝ` at $k = 2$ and `ℝ` at $k = 1$; the generalization replaces the two bespoke
model-source interfaces by one over a finite-dimensional vector space, but it does not
justify deleting the old helper engines without a consumer census. The pinned plane
engine simultaneously removes derivative defects **and collisions** (Immersion/Relative.lean:1195);
its patch step returns a closed embedding on the new compact (Immersion/Relative.lean:1493).
For distinct core points $x,y$, the collision equation imposes $n$ independent affine
conditions on $A$ since $x-y\ne0$. Its incidence dimension is $2k+kn-n$, below $kn$
when $2k<n$. This accounts for the compatibility bound $2k+1\leq n$ for simultaneous
injectivity and immersion, unlike the immersion-only count above.

**Typed dependency work remains open:** the general rank-stratum incidence/projection
lemmas (or equivalent explicit bad-parameter parametrizations), the collision estimate
if the stronger patch output is retained, and the compact patch induction must have
exact typed interfaces before implementation. General smooth-image `dimH` bounds alone
do not certify those inputs. G-E2 retains its stated model-source signature; arbitrary
source manifolds and deletion of the bespoke curve/plane helpers are deferred.

**Embedding from immersion (already general in the code).** For $2k < n$: after T8-first-half,
$f$ is an immersion on the compact $K$; the double-point set
$D := \{(x, y) \in K \times K : x \neq y,\ f(x) = f(y)\}$ is compact: an immersion is locally
injective, so the diagonal has an open $f$-nonintersecting neighborhood in $K \times K$, and
$D$ is closed in the compact $K \times K$ minus it. The double points
are removed using finitely many separating source patches. At a double point $(x,y)$,
injectivity on $K\cap C$ ensures that at least one foot is outside $C$; choose a cutoff
vanishing on $C$ with different values at the two feet. Perturb the map in a target chart
by that source-dependent cutoff. This is **not** postcomposition of the entire map by
one ambient diffeomorphism, which would preserve all double points. The collision
parameter set on separated pairs has dimension at most $2k<n$, so arbitrarily small
parameters avoid it. The finite patch induction preserves derivative injectivity and
previous separation conditions; a near-diagonal neighborhood stays collision-free by
uniform local injectivity on the compact. This is the separating-cutoff construction
at Immersion/Relative.lean:1973. No finiteness of the individual set of double points is assumed. $\square$

## 11. Maps into spheres of larger dimension (T9)

*Proof.* Let $X$ be a compact manifold, $\dim X < n$, $f \colon X \to S^n$ continuous.
(1) Regard $f$ as a map into $\mathbb R^{n+1}$. Mathlib's vector-valued smooth
approximation theorem gives a smooth $g$ with $\|g(x)-f(x)\|<1$ everywhere. Since
$\|f(x)\|=1$, $g$ never vanishes; normalize it to $\widetilde f=g/\|g\|$.
The segment from $f(x)$ to $g(x)$ stays nonzero, so normalization of that segment
supplies a homotopy from $f$ to $\widetilde f$. This is
`exists_smoothSphereRepresentative` (SH 4418–4461), using
`Continuous.exists_contMDiff_approx` at SH 4428, not the general manifold-target
smoothing provider.
(2) $\widetilde f$ is not surjective: its image has $\dim_H \leq \dim X < n$, hence is null and has empty
interior in $S^n$ (the $\dim_H$ count of §2 — no Sard needed here). Pick
$p \notin \mathrm{range}\, \widetilde f$.
(3) $S^n \setminus \{p\}$ is contractible (stereographic projection to $\mathbb{R}^n$, then
the radial contraction), so $\widetilde f$ is nullhomotopic. $\square$

## 12. Finite transverse intersections (T10)

*Proof.* Let $z \in \mathrm{range}\, F \cap \mathrm{range}\, G$, $z = F(x) = G(y)$.
Transversality $\mathrm{im}\, dF_x + \mathrm{im}\, dG_y = T_z M$ with
$\dim N + \dim P = \dim M$ makes the sum direct: $T_z M = \mathrm{im}\, dF_x \oplus
\mathrm{im}\, dG_y$. The two embedded sheets are locally closed submanifolds; choose a chart at
$z$ in which the two sheets are complementary coordinate slices (the *crossing chart*, explicit:
first straighten the $F$-sheet by a submanifold chart with
$F$-sheet $= \mathbb{R}^{\dim N} \times \{0\}$; the direct-sum decomposition says the
$G$-sheet's tangent at $z$ projects isomorphically onto the $\{0\} \times \mathbb{R}^{\dim P}$
factor, so near $z$ the $G$-sheet is a graph $\{(h(b), b)\}$ over that factor, and the shear
$(a, b) \mapsto (a - h(b), b)$ straightens it while preserving the $F$-sheet). In this chart the intersection is the single point $z$: intersections
are isolated. Hence the intersection set is discrete; it is also compact (closed inside the
compact range $F$); a discrete compact set is finite. $\square$

## 12a. Embedded arcs avoiding a finite set (E14)

Let $x\ne y$ lie in the same path component of a boundaryless manifold of dimension
at least 2, and let $S$ be finite. The conclusion is a smooth map $f:\mathbb R\to N$
whose restriction to $[0,1]$ is a closed embedding, whose derivative is injective on
that interval, whose endpoints are $x,y$, and whose interior avoids $S$. Endpoints
are allowed to belong to $S$.

First construct a short embedded arc $g$ starting at $x$ in the open set
$N\setminus(S\setminus\{x\})$. The source starts from the constant curve, repairs
its endpoint derivative while retaining its value, then uses local injectivity and
openness of derivative injectivity near zero. A small positive linear rescaling of
the parameter places all of $[0,1]$ in that neighborhood (SH 8654–8714). This is
an endpoint-local construction, not the $n\geq3$ global curve-immersion theorem.
Write $z=g(1)$; injectivity and avoidance give $z\ne x$ and $z\notin S$.

Set $C=(S\cup\{x\})\setminus\{y\}$. Join $z$ to $y$ by reversing the short
arc and following the given path from $x$ to $y$. The live implementation of the
finite-avoidance step is **E5 map general position**, not a bespoke detour:
`exists_smooth_path_avoiding_finite` (Immersion/Relative.lean:343–384) models the
finite forbidden set as a zero-dimensional manifold, takes a smooth connecting
curve, and applies the general-position disjunction on the interval with endpoints
fixed, using $1 + 0 < n$. (A punctured-ball detour — in a small chart a punctured
ball is path connected, so the path can be rerouted around each forbidden point —
is an alternative ordinary-mathematics argument for the same step; if it is retained
as the extracted route it must specify replacing the subpath between first entry
and last exit of a small ball around each forbidden point, since infinitely many
visits do not justify an unexplained finite replacement count.) Homogeneity
along this avoiding path gives a diffeomorphism $d$ moving $z$ to $y$ and fixing
$C$ pointwise (`exists_pointMoving_fixing_finite`, Immersion/Relative.lean:385–394,
which runs homogeneity in the complement). Then $f=d\circ g$
fixes the initial endpoint $x$, has final endpoint $y$, and remains an embedded
immersion. An interior point cannot map to $y$ by injectivity; if it mapped to
$S\setminus\{y\}\subset C$, fixedness and injectivity of $d$ would force $g$
to hit that same forbidden point — and if the forced hit is $x$ itself (which may
belong to $S$), $g$ still avoids $x$ on the open interval by injectivity and
$g(0)=x$ (the source makes this case explicit at SH 8774–8779).
Thus the interior avoids $S$ (SH 8715–8780).
Compactness of $[0,1]$ and Hausdorffness give the closed-embedding conclusion.

This recovers the two E14 headline outputs. Endpoint-germ, return-arc and circle-gluing
clusters are not silently included: their complete helper/output census is deferred.
