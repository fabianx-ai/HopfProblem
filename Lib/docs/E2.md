# Axis 1 — the textbook proofs

## 1. The theorems

Fix the standing generality: manifolds are smooth ($C^\infty$), modeled on
finite-dimensional real vector spaces via `ModelWithCorners`, and *boundaryless* unless
explicitly stated; "dimension" means finite $\mathbb{R}$-dimension of the model. For maps
$f \colon X \to N$, $g \colon Y \to N$ between manifolds, write $f \pitchfork g$
(*transverse at $(x, y)$*) for: $f(x) = g(y)$ implies
$\mathrm{im}\, df_x + \mathrm{im}\, dg_y = T_{f(x)} N$; and $f \pitchfork g$ for: this holds at
all pairs.

**(T1) Sard's theorem (equal-dimensional form; Hirsch Ch. 3 Thm. 1.3 pattern).** For a smooth
map $f \colon X \to F$ from an $n$-manifold to an $n$-dimensional vector space, the set of
regular values is comeager and co-null: there is a Haar-null set $T \subseteq F$ such that at
every $x$ with $f(x) \notin T$, $df_x$ is surjective.

**(T2) Parametric transversality (translation form; Hirsch Ch. 3 Thm. 2.1).** For smooth
$f \colon X \to F$, $g \colon Y \to F$ into a vector space with
$\dim X + \dim Y = \dim F$, the set of translation parameters $a \in F$ for which
$f \pitchfork (g + a)$ is dense (indeed co-null).

**(T3) Ambient transversality (Hirsch Ch. 3 Thm. 2.5 form).** Let $X$ be compact, $Y$ compact,
$N$ boundaryless, $f \colon X \to N$, $g \colon Y \to N$ smooth, with
$\dim X + \dim Y = \dim N$. Then there is a diffeomorphism $e \colon N \to N$ isotopic to the
identity (through diffeomorphisms, smoothly in time) such that $(e \circ f) \pitchfork g$.

**(T4) General position (disjunction).** If $\dim X + \dim Y < \dim N$, $X$ compact, then every
smooth $f \colon X \to N$ is homotopic rel any closed set $C$ already avoiding $g$ to a smooth
$f'$ with $\mathrm{range}\, f' \cap \mathrm{range}\, g = \varnothing$; and in the ambient form
(T3 applied after padding the domain by a sphere factor): there is an ambient diffeomorphism
isotopic to the identity with $\mathrm{range}(e \circ f) \cap \mathrm{range}\, g =
\varnothing$.

**(T5) Compactly supported isotopy extension (Hirsch Ch. 8 Thm. 1.3).** A smooth isotopy of an
open subset $U \subseteq M$ that is stationary outside a compact $K \subseteq U$ extends to a
compactly supported ambient isotopy of $M$ whenever $U$ is carried into $M$ by a chart
embedding; globally: a compactly supported isotopy of a compact submanifold extends to a
compactly supported ambient isotopy of $M$.

**(T6) The disc theorem (Milnor TDV §2 Lemma; h-cobordism Thm. 9.6).** Let $M$ be a manifold,
$D$ a finite-dimensional inner-product space with $\dim D \leq \dim M$, and let
$f, g \colon D \to M$ be smooth, injective with injective derivatives on the closed unit ball,
with $f(0) = g(0)$. Then there is a diffeomorphism $P$ of $M$, isotopic to the identity, with
$P \circ f = g$ on the unit ball. (In the code the ambient is compact and
$\dim D < \dim M$; the proof needs $2 \leq \dim M$.)

**(T7) Homogeneity.** A connected boundaryless manifold is homogeneous under compactly
supported diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity: for $x, y$ in one connected component and
any neighborhood $U$ of a path from $x$ to $y$, there is such a diffeomorphism moving $x$ to
$y$ and fixed outside $U$.

**(T8) Relative Whitney immersion and embedding (Hirsch Ch. 2 Thms. 1.1, 1.3).** Let $X$ be a
$k$-manifold, $N$ an $n$-manifold, $f \colon X \to N$ smooth.
* If $2k + 1 \leq n$, then rel any closed set $C$ on which $f$ is already an immersion, $f$ is
  homotopic to an immersion; more precisely on any compact $K$: there is $g$ homotopic to $f$
  rel $C$ immersive on $K \cup L$ for a second compact $L$.
* If $2k < n$ and $f$ is an immersion on a compact $K$, injective with injective derivative on
  $K \cap C$, then $f$ is homotopic rel $C$ to $g$ which is a closed embedding on $K$ and
  immersive on $K$.
(The code carries these at $k = 2$ with source $\mathbb{R}^2$ and $n \geq 5$, and at $k = 1$
with $n \geq 3$; the second bullet exists at general $k$ already. The generalization, **G-E2**,
is the first bullet at general $k$: §10.)

**(T9) Maps into spheres of larger dimension (Milnor TDV §2 pattern).** A continuous map from a
compact manifold of dimension $< n$ to $S^n$ is nullhomotopic. In particular every map
$S^m \to S^n$ with $m < n$ is nullhomotopic.

**(T10) Finite transverse intersections.** Let $F \colon N \to M$, $G \colon P \to M$ be
embeddings of compact manifolds into a manifold with
$\dim N + \dim P = \dim M$, meeting transversely. Then $\mathrm{range}\, F \cap
\mathrm{range}\, G$ is finite.

## 2. Conventions and standing inputs

From Mathlib: measure theory (Haar-null sets, `addHaar_image_eq_zero_of_det_fderivWithin_eq_zero`
— the analytic Sard lemma), the manifold library (`ModelWithCorners`, `ContMDiff`, `mfderiv`,
`Diffeomorph`, `PartialDiffeomorph`), Hausdorff dimension (`dimH`) and its monotonicity under
smooth maps. From GLM's lanes D1/D2 (not yet landed; used from `Hopf/` until then, recorded as
a seam): the tubular neighborhood theorem and the Whitney approximation theorem
(`exists_contMDiff_approx`: a continuous map into a manifold, smooth on a neighborhood of a
closed set, is homotopic rel that set to a smooth map). $\mathcal{H}$-dimension facts: a smooth
image of a $d$-manifold has $\dim_H \leq d$; a subset of an $n$-manifold with $\dim_H < n$ has
empty interior and dense complement.

## 3. Sard's theorem (T1)

*Proof.* Cover the domain by countably many charts (Lindelöf); a countable union of null sets
is null, so it suffices to prove: for a smooth $f \colon U \to \mathbb{R}^n$ with
$U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ open, the set of critical values is null. The critical set is
$\{x : \det df_x = 0\}$; on it, the analytic Sard lemma
(`addHaar_image_eq_zero_of_det_fderivWithin_eq_zero`: the image under a differentiable map of a
set where the derivative's determinant vanishes is Haar-null) gives nullness of the image
directly; the complement is the claimed co-null set of regular values, and regular-value sets
are automatically open-dense... (density: a co-null set in a space with a nonzero-on-opens
Haar measure is dense). $\square$

*Remark.* The manifold statement quantifies over an open $s \subseteq X$ and concludes
surjectivity of `mfderiv`; the chart form quantifies over differentiability on a set. Both are
the equal-dimensional case; the general Sard theorem ($\dim X > \dim F$ allowed) is not needed
by this lane and is not proved here.

## 4. Parametric transversality (T2)

*Proof.* Let $D := \dim X$, $Z := \dim Y$, $D + Z = \dim F =: m$. Consider the sheet-difference
map $\Phi \colon X \times Y \to F$, $\Phi(x, y) = g(y) - f(x)$. A point $(x, y)$ is critical
for $\Phi$ iff $df_x$ and $dg_y$ fail to jointly span — more precisely
$d\Phi_{(x,y)} = dg_y - df_x$ (as a map $T_xX \times T_yY \to F$) is surjective iff
$\mathrm{im}\, df_x + \mathrm{im}\, dg_y = F$, which is exactly transversality at $(x, y)$.
Now apply Sard (T1) to $\Phi$ (dimension check: $\dim(X \times Y) = D + Z = m = \dim F$): for
co-null-many $a \in F$, $a$ is a regular value of $\Phi$. But $a$ a regular value with
$\dim(X \times Y) = \dim F$ means: at every $(x, y)$ with $\Phi(x, y) = a$, $d\Phi$ is
surjective; and $\Phi(x, y) = a$ iff $g(y) = f(x) + a$. So for co-null — hence dense — many
$a$, the translates $f + a$ and $g$ (equivalently $f$ and $g - a$) are everywhere transverse.
$\square$

## 5. Ambient transversality (T3)

*Proof.* The construction has three layers.

*The bump family.* In a chart ball $B \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, let $\beta \colon
\mathbb{R}^n \to [0, 1]$ be smooth, compactly supported, $\equiv 1$ on a smaller ball $B'$.
For a parameter $a \in \mathbb{R}^n$ define the compactly supported diffeomorphism
$e_a(y) = y + \beta(y) \cdot a$; for $\|a\|$ small (less than the distance scale
$(\sup \|d\beta\|)^{-1}$), $e_a$ is a diffeomorphism (its derivative is $\mathrm{id} +
a \otimes d\beta$, invertible for small $a$), and $t \mapsto e_{ta}$ is a smooth isotopy from
$\mathrm{id}$ to $e_a$. Transported through a chart $\varphi \colon U \to \mathbb{R}^n$ of $N$
and extended by the identity, this gives a compactly supported ambient isotopy class on $N$,
fixed outside $\varphi^{-1}(\mathrm{supp}\,\beta)$.

*The plateau lemma.* Let $K \subseteq X$ be compact and $f(K)$ contained in the plateau
$\{\beta = 1\}$ of one chart. On that plateau $e_a(f(x)) = f(x) + a$ in chart coordinates, so
transversality of $(e_a \circ f)$ against $g$ at points of $K$ is exactly transversality of the
translated map — which holds for dense (co-null) small $a$ by parametric transversality (T4
applied in the chart; the hypothesis $\dim X + \dim Y = \dim N$ is the dimension equation).
Choose such an $a$ with $\|a\| < \varepsilon$.

*The patch induction.* Cover the compact $X$ by finitely many patch interiors, each the
plateau of a bump in a chart. Apply the plateau lemma successively: after step $i$,
transversality holds on $K_1 \cup \dots \cup K_i$; step $i+1$ perturbs by a bump supported so
close to $K_{i+1}$ and so small that (openness of the transversality condition on compacts —
the surjectivity of the coproduct derivative is open and uniform on the compact
$K_1 \cup \dots \cup K_i$) the previous region stays transverse. After finitely many steps,
compose the small diffeomorphisms: the composite $e$ is isotopic to the identity (concatenate
the isotopies) and $(e \circ f) \pitchfork g$ everywhere. $\square$

*Corollary (disjunction, the second half of T4).* If $\dim X + \dim Y < \dim N$, pad: apply T3
to $f \circ \mathrm{pr}_1 \colon X \times S^d \to N$ (with $d$ chosen so the dimensions match,
$\dim X + d + \dim Y = \dim N$, possible since the sphere of any dimension is available; the
padded map is constant in the sphere factor, so its transversality against $g$ is equivalent to
disjointness of the ranges — the coproduct derivative can never be surjective at a meeting
point, since the image lands in a $\dim X$-dimensional direction set). Transversality of the
padded pair is vacuous, hence the ranges of $e \circ f$ and $g$ are disjoint. $\square$

## 6. General position, map form (T4, first half)

*Proof.* Work in patches as in §5 but perturb $f$ itself: it suffices (patch induction as
before, using a homotopy through the perturbations to keep the rel-$C$ condition) to perturb
$f$ on one chart compactly, by $f'(x) = f(x) + \beta(x) \cdot a$ in chart coordinates, keeping
$f' = f$ off the patch (so the rel-$C$ condition is preserved: on $C$ no change happens where
$C$ meets the patch — ensure the patch is disjoint from $C$ or $\beta$ vanishes near $C$; the
hypothesis "range $g$ already avoided on $C$" gives room, since avoidance is open). The
condition $f'(x) \notin \mathrm{range}\, g$ is: $a \neq$ (something in the $g$-image
difference) — the set of bad parameters is the image under the smooth map
$(x, y) \mapsto g(y) - f(x)$ on the patch of the... it is contained in
$\mathrm{range}\, \Phi_\beta$ where $\Phi_\beta(x, y) = (\beta(x))^{-1}(g(y) - f(x))$-type
difference; crudest form: the bad set is contained in the image of
$X \times Y \to F$ up to the cutoff, and $\dim(X \times Y) = \dim X + \dim Y < \dim N$: a
smooth image of a lower-dimensional manifold has $\dim_H < n$, hence empty interior, hence
dense complement. So arbitrarily small good $a$ exist. $\square$

## 7. Compactly supported isotopy extension (T5)

*Proof (the form the code proves).* Let $\Phi \colon U \to V$ be a chart diffeomorphism between
opens of manifolds $X, Y$ (a `PartialDiffeomorph`), and $A \colon \mathbb{R} \times U \to U$ a
smooth isotopy of the identity through diffeomorphisms onto open images, stationary outside a
compact $K \subseteq U$. Define $B(t, y) = \Phi(A(t, \Phi^{-1} y))$ for
$y \in \Phi(\text{support})$, and $B(t, y) = y$ outside. Smoothness holds because the two
definitions agree on the overlap (on a neighborhood of the boundary of the support, $A$ is the
identity): this is the only point where the support hypothesis enters. Each $B(t, \cdot)$ is a
diffeomorphism of $Y$ (identity outside a compact in $V$, transported inside), $B(0) =
\mathrm{id}$, and $B$ conjugates $A$ through $\Phi$ by construction.

*The classical form*: an isotopy of a compact submanifold $i_t \colon P \to M$ with compact
support in time extends to an ambient compactly supported isotopy. Proof sketch by the standard
covering: the track of the isotopy is covered by finitely many charts in which the moving
submanifold is a coordinate slice (tubular/normal charts — lane D2 input); extend chart by chart
with the construction above, pushing the support forward; compactness of $P$ and of the time
interval make the induction finite. (The code's content is the chart-transport engine plus the
supported-relative-isotopy structure packaging; the global statement is assembled at the
consumer sites. The Lib file records both.) $\square$

## 8. The disc theorem (T6)

*Proof.* Let $f, g \colon D \to M$ be as stated, $k = \dim D$, $n = \dim M \geq 2$ (and
$k < n$ in the application), $f(0) = g(0) = m$.

*Step 1 (reduce to a common chart).* Choose a chart $\varphi$ at $m$; after shrinking the disks
(precompose with a radial homothety $x \mapsto \varepsilon x$; the shrinking is achieved by an
isotopy: the radial family $R_t(x) = \varphi(t \cdot \|x\|^2) \cdot x$ for a suitable bump
$\varphi$ is a diffeomorphism isotopy moving the unit disk to the $\varepsilon$-disk, fixed
outside a slightly larger ball — the disk-shrinking lemma), both images lie in the chart. So
wlog $M = \mathbb{R}^n$ and $f, g$ are disk embeddings near $0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with
$f(0) = g(0) = 0$.

*Step 2 (align the derivative frames).* The derivatives $df_0, dg_0 \colon \mathbb{R}^k
\to \mathbb{R}^n$ are injective; extend the two frames to bases. There is a linear isotopy
between the two extended frames *provided the orientations agree on the determinant sign*:
$\mathrm{GL}^+(n)$ is connected (every determinant-$1$ matrix is a product of transvections,
each transvection $A_s = I + s\, E_{ij}$ is connected to $I$ by the path $s \mapsto A_s$;
positive determinant scales to $1$), and the two bases can be pre-adjusted by a reflection in
the *ambient* complement (available since $k < n$: the normal space is nonzero, flip one normal
vector) so the determinants match. Realize the frame isotopy by a compactly supported
diffeomorphism isotopy of $\mathbb{R}^n$ fixing $0$ (integrate the time-dependent linear map
against a cutoff — the realized-germ machinery: a germ of a diffeomorphism at $0$ that is
isotopic to the identity through germs with the same $1$-jet variation is realized by a
compactly supported diffeomorphism). After applying it to $g$, we have $df_0 = dg_0$.

*Step 3 (match the germs).* With the same derivative at $0$, the two embeddings differ by
$o(\|x\|)$; the interpolation $h_t(x) = (1 - t) f(x) + t\, g(x)$ is an embedding on a small
ball for all $t$ (the derivative $dh_t$ at $0$ is $df_0 = dg_0 \neq$ degenerate, uniformly for
small $\|x\|$ by the $o(\|x\|)$ bound), so after shrinking again, $f$ and $g$ are connected by
an isotopy of embeddings with compact support in the chart; extend by the identity (T5).

*Step 4 (assemble).* The three isotopies (shrink, frame alignment, germ interpolation), each
compactly supported and isotopic to the identity, compose. $\square$

## 9. Homogeneity (T7)

*Proof.* For $x \in U \subseteq M$ ($U$ open), the isotopic-orbit
$\{y : \exists$ compactly supported diffeomorphism isotopic to the identity, fixed outside $U$,
moving $x$ to $y\}$ is open: for $y$ near $x$, a chart ball around both and the bump isotopy of
§5 (with $a = y - x$ in chart coordinates) moves $x$ to $y$. So the orbit is open; orbits of
this equivalence relation partition $M$ into open sets. A connected (even: preconnected, or
path-connected via a path $\gamma$) manifold has one orbit: along a path, cover by finitely
many such balls and compose. The support stays inside any open $U$ containing the path by
choosing the balls inside $U$. $\square$

## 10. Relative Whitney immersion and embedding (T8) — the generalization G-E2

This is the one place where the formalization is dimension-pinned and the textbook must supply
the general argument. The pinned engine is the affine-perturbation density lemma for maps of
the plane; the general statement and proof:

**Lemma (immersion creation, general $k$).** *Let $X$ be a $k$-manifold, $N$ an $n$-manifold
with $2k + 1 \leq n$, $f \colon X \to N$ smooth, $K, L \subseteq X$ compact, $C \subseteq X$
closed, $f$ immersive on (a neighborhood of) $K \cap C$-side… precisely: $f$ has injective
derivative on $K$; then there is $g$, homotopic to $f$ rel $C$ (with $K \cap C \subseteq
\mathrm{int}\, C$-type hygiene), immersive on $K \cup L$.*

*Proof.* Cover $L$ by finitely many chart patches compactly contained in chart domains; it
suffices to create the immersion property on one patch $P$ while keeping it on $K$ (open
condition, uniform on compacts) and not disturbing $C$. On the patch, in chart coordinates,
consider the affine perturbation family
$$g_a(x) = f(x) + \beta(x)\, A(x - x_0), \qquad a = A \in \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{R}^k,
\mathbb{R}^n),$$
with $\beta$ a cutoff $\equiv 1$ on the patch core, $x_0$ its center. Then
$dg_{a,x} = df_x + \beta(x) A + (d\beta_x) A (x - x_0)$; on the core this is $df_x + A +
O(\|x - x_0\|)$. The set of matrices $A$ for which $df_x + A$ fails to be injective at some
core point $x$ is contained in a finite union of submanifold images of codimension
$n - k + 1$ (the non-injective $k$-frames in $\mathbb{R}^n$ form the Stiefel stratification:
$\mathrm{Hom}$-matrices of rank $< k$ have codimension $\geq n - k + 1$ in
$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{R}^k, \mathbb{R}^n)$; pulling back along the smooth evaluation
$(x) \mapsto df_x$-translate family over the $k$-dimensional core drops the codimension count
to: bad set of parameters has Hausdorff dimension $\leq k + (kn - (n - k + 1)) < kn$ —
i.e. it is null in the parameter space $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{R}^k, \mathbb{R}^n)$ — provided
$k < n - k + 1$, i.e. $2k + 1 \leq n$). So for a dense (co-null) set of small $A$, the
perturbation is immersive on the core. Smallness keeps the immersion on $K$ (open, uniform on
compact) and the homotopy class (straight-line homotopy $f + t(\beta A(\cdot - x_0))$), and
the cutoff makes it rel $C$. Finite induction over patches. $\square$

*Sanity check against the pins:* $k = 1$: $2 \cdot 1 + 1 = 3 \leq n$ — the curve chain's
`3 ≤ finrank ℝ G`. $k = 2$: $5 \leq n$ — the plane chain's `5 ≤ finrank ℝ G`. The
$k$-dimensional source in the code is the model space $\mathbb{R}^k$ written
`Plane := ℝ × ℝ` at $k = 2$ and `ℝ` at $k = 1$; the generalization replaces the two bespoke
engines by one over `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin k)` (equivalently any $k$-dimensional model), with
the same proofs and the codimension count carried by `dimH` lemmas that already exist at
general dimension.

**Embedding from immersion (already general in the code).** For $2k < n$: after T8-first-half,
$f$ is an immersion on the compact $K$; the double-point set
$\{(x, y) \in K \times K : x \neq y,\ f(x) = f(y)\}$ is compact (immersion + compactness) and
is removed pair by pair: at each double point $(x, y)$ choose a separating patch isotopy moving
one sheet off the other (possible without new intersections since the expected dimension of new
double points created by a small perturbation is $2k - n < 0$ — the perturbation family kills a
chosen double point while avoiding new ones on a compact: the same codimension count as above
at codimension $n - 2k \geq 1$); finitely many steps over a compact cover of the double-point
set. $\square$

## 11. Maps into spheres of larger dimension (T9)

*Proof.* Let $X$ be a compact manifold, $\dim X < n$, $f \colon X \to S^n$ continuous.
(1) Approximate $f$ by a smooth map $\widetilde f$ homotopic to it (Whitney approximation;
lane D1/D2 input — the smooth approximation theorem, `exists_contMDiff_approx`).
(2) $\widetilde f$ is not surjective: its image has $\dim_H \leq \dim X < n$, hence empty
interior in $S^n$ (equivalently Sard (T1-style dimension count): a smooth map from a
lower-dimensional manifold has all values critical, so its image is null). Pick
$p \notin \mathrm{range}\, \widetilde f$.
(3) $S^n \setminus \{p\}$ is contractible (stereographic projection to $\mathbb{R}^n$, then
the radial contraction), so $\widetilde f$ is nullhomotopic. $\square$

## 12. Finite transverse intersections (T10)

*Proof.* Let $z \in \mathrm{range}\, F \cap \mathrm{range}\, G$, $z = F(x) = G(y)$.
Transversality $\mathrm{im}\, dF_x + \mathrm{im}\, dG_y = T_z M$ with
$\dim N + \dim P = \dim M$ makes the sum direct: $T_z M = \mathrm{im}\, dF_x \oplus
\mathrm{im}\, dG_y$. The two embedded sheets are locally closed submanifolds; choose a chart at
$z$ in which the two sheets are complementary coordinate slices (the *crossing chart*: the
direct sum decomposition gives an isomorphism $T_z M \cong T_x N \oplus T_y P$, and straightening
both sheets simultaneously — the inverse function theorem applied to the combined
parametrization $N \times P \to M$, $(u, v) \mapsto$ (flow/pushout construction) — yields a
chart with $F$-sheet $= \mathbb{R}^{\dim N} \times \{0\}$ and $G$-sheet $= \{0\} \times
\mathbb{R}^{\dim P}$). In this chart the intersection is the single point $z$: intersections
are isolated. Hence the intersection set is discrete; it is also compact (closed inside the
compact range $F$); a discrete compact set is finite. $\square$
